The "Income" column includes capital gains + dividends/interest for tax purposes, but cash inflow each year = annuity + Social Security + gross RMD only (cap gains/dividends are treated as staying invested, not spendable cash, though tax on them is still deducted from cash). NJ tax uses its own fixed (non-inflated) brackets, $4,000 total exemption ($2,000 base + $1,000/spouse for both being 65+), a deduction for unreimbursed medical expenses over 2% of NJ gross income, and taxes conversions as ordinary income. IRMAA surcharge is a real Medicare Part B+D premium add-on (both spouses on Medicare, so doubled) based on MAGI from 2 years earlier — e.g. 2026 MAGI affects 2028 cash flow, not 2026. The surcharge dollar amounts inflate at ~6%/yr (historical Medicare premium growth), separate from the ~2.8% CPI used for the MAGI tier thresholds. NIIT (3.8% federal) applies to the lesser of investment income or the amount MAGI exceeds $250K (fixed threshold, not inflation-indexed) — conversions and RMDs aren't NIIT income themselves but can trigger it by pushing MAGI over the line.
